![Fig 1. Bearing & Distance from the vessel: the line, the range ring and the readout](bearing-distance-1.jpg)

Find the bearing and distance between two points on the chart — from your boat to
a headland, from a light to a hazard — with a ring showing everything else at that
same range.

**From your boat:** right-click the chart (press and hold on a touchscreen) and
choose **Bearing & Distance**. A line runs from your vessel to that point. As the
boat moves, the line and the numbers follow it.

**Between any two points:** open the **Draw** menu and choose **Bearing &
Distance**. Click or tap the start point. With a mouse, the line and ring follow
the pointer so you can sweep around until you find what you're after; click to
place the end point. On a touchscreen, tap the end point — dragging the chart
still just pans it.

The label at the end point shows the **distance**, the **bearing** to it, and the
**reciprocal** bearing (marked *recip*). The reciprocal is the bearing back from
the end point, which is what you plot when you take a bearing on a light or a
landmark and want a position line from it.

The thin **range ring** is centered on the start point and passes through the end
point, so anything on the ring is the same distance away. It's handy for checking
swing room at anchor or how far off a hazard you'll pass. The ring is unfilled so
the chart underneath stays readable, and it's drawn as a true equal-distance
circle, so it stays accurate at high latitudes where a flat circle on the chart
would be distorted.

Drag the end-point handle to move it; the line, ring and readout follow.

A placed measurement stays on the chart until you close it with **✕**. The **➤**
button on the label starts navigating to the end point.

Distances use your unit preferences.

For a multi-leg distance, such as along a planned path, use **Measure** in the
Draw menu instead.
